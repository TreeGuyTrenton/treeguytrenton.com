Wildfire preparedness

Defensible space, explained.

Defensible space is the managed area around your home that slows a wildfire down — and gives firefighters somewhere safe to work. It matters here: lodgepole pine makes up most of Summit County’s forest, and it burns readily.

0–5 ft

Immediate zone

Closest to the house. Keep it non-combustible: clear needles and debris from the roof and gutters, screen vents, and move anything flammable away from walls, decks, and porches.

5–30 ft

Intermediate zone

Break up the fuel. Prune branches roughly 6–10 ft off the ground, leave space between tree crowns, keep the canopy back from the house, and use drives and patios as natural fuel breaks.

30–100 ft

Extended zone

Slow the fire before it arrives. Clear heavy ground litter and dead material, thin the small conifers coming up between mature trees, and widen the spacing between canopies as you move out.

Under Colorado’s HB25-1182, the mitigation work you do can also factor into your wildfire risk score and your homeowner’s coverage. Your fire district offers a free assessment — and we’ll walk the property with you before anything gets cut.
